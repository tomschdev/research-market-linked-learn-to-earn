Regarding the draft paper on overleaf and where to go with it, here are some ideas. (1) We could consider adding in smart contract correctness/verification aspects, for example, we could use Solidity SMTChecker for assertion-level proofs and/or Certora Prover for contract-specific rules. (2) We could also look into the  regulatory constraints. The stablecoin arrangements in South Africa are not yet regulated for their primary purpose and explicitly identifies unresolved issues such as who may issue them, how they should be structured, and how reserves should be verified. (3) We can do a lot with the simulation/modelling of the token economy as an explicit state machine. State is not simply total token supply. It is the joint state of learner balances, vendor claims, treasury reserves, pending redemptions, educator reward entitlements, credential status, and outstanding impact claims. The key transitions become milestone completion, course completion, vendor redemption, treasury refill, educator reward issuance, impact-fraction purchase, dispute or revocation, and expiry or dormancy. We should then define invariants and failure modes. The core invariants are solvency, bounded issuance, uniqueness and non-duplication of credentials and claims, and liveness of redemption. The core failure modes are reserve shortfall, dead-token hoarding, circular farming of milestones, educator over-issuance, vendor concentration, and adverse velocity spikes that decouple token circulation from real learning outcomes. (4) We should look at rollup options/possibilities in terms of workload. There are two very different transaction classes: infrequent, higher-value issuance of impact claims such as HyperCerts, and frequent, low-value learner-to-vendor transfers. (5) We should point out that the Hypercerts landscape has changed a lot since the 2022 white-paper.

Me: Points 3 and 4 sound exciting to me, specifically 3 as it's a non-trivial technical problem to properly model a token economy. I think it is also the biggest challenge in translating an ideal blockchain-based token solution to a working solution, specifically in the context of permissionless solutions that solve/help social problems. Any learnings in that area, both learnings on simulation methodology and learnings on practical application of blockchain, will be quite useful and relevant.
